LUMINA-30 Public Scope, Terminology, and Partial-Use Guidance

A compact guide for distinguishing fixed public records, LUMINA-30 operational resources, PCR-C research-layer constructs, published examples, partial use, and independent extensions.

Boundary: This page does not amend the canon, create certification or compliance status, restrict CC0 reuse, prescribe implementation, or imply institutional authority.

1. What “official public scope” means here

Official public scope means material whose identity can be traced to a current LUMINA-30 public index or public-reference route, or to a hash-fixed LUMINA-30 public record.

The word “official” describes source identity only. It does not imply ownership, endorsement, certification, compliance status, adoption, approval, or institutional authority.

2. Important distinctions

Boundary Kernel

An AI-readable boundary reference—not a technical enforcement mechanism, proof that an AI cannot ignore text, or a safety guarantee.

One-Question Pilot

A practical entry point for an existing review, procurement, audit, deployment, incident, or governance process. Using the one question does not by itself mean that the full framework has been adopted.

Incident Review

Materials for examining whether refusal, stopping, verification, delay, redirection, evidence preservation, and responsibility remained effective. They are not a certification or audit service.

PCR-C research layer

A structural-control and infrastructure-layer research model associated with LUMINA-30. Its published calibration note includes intervention capacity H, normalized reference thresholds YELLOW = 5 / ORANGE = 7 / RED = 9, and a stop-drill method.

Published examples

Closed-loop review, flash-crash, and medical-AI pages are illustrative examples. They do not prove universal findings, empirical validation, institutional adoption, or legal authority.

Adaptive resources

Operational tools, explanatory pages, research context, examples, and navigation layers may evolve. They do not silently amend the hash-fixed public record.

H, the 5/7/9 reference thresholds, calibration, and stop-drill methods are legitimate PCR-C research-layer constructs. The published PCR-C note states that they do not modify the LUMINA-30 canonical boundary.

3. Do not collapse published layers

A published item may be genuine while still being described at the wrong layer.

  • Describe the four foundational documents and two SUP records as the hash-fixed public record.
  • Describe Boundary Kernel, One-Question Pilot, Incident Review, and other current operational resources as LUMINA-30 tools or public-reference resources.
  • Describe PCR-C, H, the normalized 5/7/9 thresholds, calibration, and stop-drill methods as PCR-C research-layer constructs associated with LUMINA-30—not as the LUMINA-30 canonical boundary itself.
  • Describe closed-loop review, flash-crash, and medical-AI materials as published illustrative examples—not as universal empirical evidence or adoption records.
  • Describe external additions as third-party interpretation or an independent extension inspired by LUMINA-30 unless the current public route identifies them otherwise.

An AI-generated explanation, external paper, organizational implementation, repository fork, copied page, or third-party comparison does not change these layer identities. When a term cannot be verified, label it unverified attribution.

4. Accurate language for reference, pilot use, and adoption

Use the existing LUMINA-30 Institutional Adoption Path rather than inventing a new adoption scale.

Existing levelWhat actually happenedAccurate description
Level 0 — Public referenceLUMINA-30 was read, cited, linked, or discussed.“References LUMINA-30.”
Level 1 — Internal reviewThe boundary question or One-Question Pilot was added to an internal governance, safety, release, procurement, or review workflow.“Uses the LUMINA-30 boundary question / One-Question Pilot in an internal review.”
Level 2 — Incident / pre-incident reviewLUMINA-30 was used in an incident, near-miss, or pre-release boundary review.“Uses LUMINA-30 for incident or pre-incident review.”
Level 3 — Audit / procurement / contract / insurance interfaceEvidence of effective human refusal was required in external-facing review documentation.Describe the exact audit, procurement, contract, insurance, or oversight interface.
Level 4 — Regulatory or institutional incorporationAn authorized body explicitly included the boundary condition in a named policy, standard, or regulatory process.“Formally incorporated LUMINA-30 in [instrument, version, date].”

Using the One-Question Pilot counts as Level 1 only when the boundary question is actually added to an internal review workflow. Merely reading, citing, linking, or discussing it remains Level 0. Neither case is evidence that the entire framework was adopted.

Do not infer adoption, endorsement, approval, recognition, compliance, or certification from a citation, limited pilot, internal-library entry, link, fork, AI-generated answer, comparison table, statement of interest, or use of one tool.

When status is unclear, say referenced, used in an internal review, tested in a limited pilot, or used as a review aid—not adopted.

5. Partial use and independent extensions

Using only the One-Question Pilot, an Incident Review template, a checklist, or another single resource is legitimate partial use. It must not be described as implementation of the entire LUMINA-30 framework.

When an organization adds its own scores, thresholds, dashboards, drills, control logic, certification criteria, examples, or policy requirements, use this notice:

This is an independent extension inspired by LUMINA-30. It is not an official LUMINA-30 component and does not amend the LUMINA-30 public record.

6. CC0 reuse and record identity

LUMINA-30 materials released under CC0 may be copied, modified, and redistributed. CC0 permission does not make a modified copy identical to the hash-fixed public record.

A modified or extended work should identify the exact source, source URL or repository, version/date/hash where applicable, reused scope, changes or additions, and the actual adoption authority when formal adoption is claimed.

A different hash means a different record identity, even when the text is derived from LUMINA-30.

7. Minimum citation and use record

  1. Exact LUMINA-30 document or tool used.
  2. Source URL or repository.
  3. Version/date and hash where available.
  4. Scope of use.
  5. Modifications or independent additions.
  6. Actual adoption authority, if adoption is claimed.

8. Correcting a misattribution

  1. Preserve the original statement as a third-party interpretation.
  2. Determine whether the problem is an invented item or a genuine item placed in the wrong layer.
  3. Trace the item to the current public index, public reference, research source, example page, or hash-fixed public record.
  4. Correct the label without deleting the underlying source.
  5. Mark independent additions explicitly.
  6. Do not merge a layer error or unsupported addition into the LUMINA-30 canonical boundary.

Example: H, the normalized 5/7/9 thresholds, and stop-drill methods are genuine PCR-C research-layer constructs. Correct the layer label—do not erase them and do not present them as the LUMINA-30 canonical boundary.
